My name is Aaron Cupp. I have been on the internet for WAY too long...

I started my journey down the rabbit hole that is internet technology when I was about 11 years old.  The internet was still GOPHER and ARCHIE access via BBS systems.   I got lucky and had access to the local college internet via my mother, who was one of the SYSOPs at the college.

I am mostly self-taught when it comes to my computer education.  I learned BASIC and COBOL on my own from books.  Self taught linux from breaking systems I built in my bedroom.  My first Linux OS was Slackware...floppy install.  There was about 50 of them for the OS and X-windows :)  I got into IRC and MUDs not long after getting online.  Both of these got me more involved in programming and systems.  I picked up LPC (a C-like language for game development) from MUD gaming.  I picked up tcl from playing with eggdrop bots in IRC.

I got my official education from Modesto Junior College, earning an A.S. in Computer Science as well as a Computer Programming Certificate.

In my spare time while working for a K-12 in Northern California I was involved in running a number of IRC networks and internet radio stations.  From 2007–2008 I developed the station for techno-dnb.com, and in 2009 I launched my own project and operated it until about 2014 as an online radio platform.  I used these personal projects as a basis for my professional advancement and moved out of the public sector and into the private world of the Bay Area tech bubble.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, I had the opportunity to start working on my personal projects again.  I relaunched my Tech-Noid Systems project as a bass music collective — part soundsystem, part open-source workshop for the tools I always wished existed.

These days Tech-Noid Systems is where my music and engineering habits collide.  On the music side the collective has a QSC-based **soundsystem** now and it's getting used — day parties around Sacramento, a monthly in the works — and I'm a regular on EMP Radio's Demo Derby and Slappy Hour shows.  [The mixes](/page/music/) are all archived.  On the engineering side it's become a home for open-source software aimed at broadcasters and streamers — like [obs-radio-output](/page/obs-radio-output/), a native OBS Studio plugin that streams straight to an internet radio server (public beta, Windows included now).

Outside the collective, I'm always building tools for the work I actually do.  [Audiophore](/page/audiophore/) is the visuals one — a low-latency bridge that drives lighting and lasers from live audio, which I'm putting to use in my own VJ sets.  The newer thread is [the bench](/page/electronics/).  Somewhere along the way the infrastructure habit went physical: I built out a microsoldering and board-repair bench — microscope, hot air, thermal camera, the works — plus [the software that runs it](/page/benchhud/) and [thirty-odd printed fixtures](/page/3d-printer-models/) that hold it all together.  The bench is [open for repair work](/page/electronics-repair/).  The whole shelf of projects is on the [Projects](/page/projects/) page.

On the professional side, the past several years took me into technology leadership — as a Manager of Site Reliability Engineering, where I hired, onboarded, and mentored a geographically distributed SRE team alongside two offshore SRE/DevOps teams supporting a SaaS data analytics platform.  Today I'm back closer to the systems themselves as a Senior Infrastructure Engineer at Feedonomics (part of Commerce) — GCP, Kubernetes, GitOps, and the audit evidence that goes with all of it.  20+ years of Linux and infrastructure work behind me, and no sign of climbing back out of the rabbit hole.

I'm also looking for what's next — senior IC or engineering leadership in infrastructure, SRE, or platform work.  The [resume](/page/resume/) is current, and the [inbox](mailto:mrcupp@mrcupp.com) is open.
