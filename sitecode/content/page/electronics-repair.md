### What gets fixed
Most "repair" is really replacement — swap the whole board, bill for the board. I'd rather
find the one part that actually failed and fix that. That's board-level repair, and it's
the whole job.

- **Laptops** — logic-board diagnosis, dead charge circuits, shorted rails, liquid damage,
  ports and connectors. The fault traced to the failed part instead of shotgunning
  replacements.
- **GPUs** — no-power, no-display, and the cards that trip a PSU the moment they're plugged
  in. Diagnosed on [a rig built for exactly that](/page/pcie-gpu-test-rig/), not in
  somebody's gaming PC.
- **DJ gear** — mixers and controllers: scratchy faders, dead pots, channels that cut out,
  the stuff that fails right before a gig.
- **Retro hardware** — restoration and recapping of older machines. The ones worth keeping
  running.
- **Microsoldering throughout** — fine-pitch and BGA rework, lifted pads and broken traces,
  jumper repairs. The small, fiddly work that needs a microscope and hot air.

### What won't get taken
Saying this up front saves us both an email:

- **Boards someone else already "fixed."** Lifted pads, blobbed jumpers, and cooked
  neighbours turn a two-hour job into a rebuild. I'll still look — just know the odds drop.
- **Anything under active warranty.** Send it back to the manufacturer. Opening it voids
  a free repair, and that's a bad trade.
- **Data recovery as the goal.** The shop fixes boards. If the data is the thing you
  actually need, a recovery lab is the right call, and going in with a soldering iron first
  can make their job harder.

### How it works
Tell the shop what you've got and you'll get a straight answer about whether it's fixable,
what it'll take, and when the answer is no. **No-fix, no-fee on diagnosis.** And the usual
caveat with any board work: back up anything you can't lose. I'll be careful, but I can't be
responsible for data on a device that's already failing.

The more you can say before anything ships, the faster that answer comes back:

- Device, and the model or board number if you have it
- What it does now, and what it should be doing
- What changed right before it failed — drop, spill, power event, a repair attempt
- What's already been tried, and whether it's been opened before
- Photos of the board, especially anything that looks burnt, corroded, or bulging
- Where you are, and whether you'd ship it or drop it off
