The bench is going on camera. Board repair is oddly watchable — a dead device comes in,
you find the one failed part under the scope, and it comes back to life. The build projects
too. It's the kind of work that's more fun to share than to do alone.

### What a stream looks like
The feed runs through [benchhud](/page/benchhud/), the heads-up display I built for the
bench, so you see what I'm seeing:

- **The scope**, at working magnification, with the **thermal overlay** registered on top
  of it — so when a shorted part starts cooking, it lights up on the board, labelled with
  its temperature.
- **Live instrument readings** — bench supply current and the meter — on screen next to the
  work, so "watch the current drop when I lift this pad" is something you can actually
  watch.
- **The whole diagnosis**, not just the fix. Finding the part is most of the job, and it's
  the part worth seeing.

### What goes on camera
Board-level repair, microsoldering, and the occasional vintage machine — the jobs from
[Repair Services](/page/electronics-repair/), plus build and bench projects when there's
something to build. If you send in a board and you'd like to watch it get fixed, say so in
the intake and I'll put it on the stream.

**Customer details never appear on stream.** The intake system that holds names and
contacts is a separate service on a separate network from the thing that composites the
video, on purpose — [the boundary is the point](/post/2026-08-10-bench-stack-privacy-boundary/).
What you'll see is a board, a job number, and the work.
